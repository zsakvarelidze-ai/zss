Atlas

The map opens at continents. Click one and it frames that continent and shows its regions; click a region and it shows the countries inside it; click a country and its own divisions are drawn below. Each click descends one segment of the code, because a code is the path down the tree — which is also why selecting a unit lights everything inside it.

    The register

    From continents down to settlements: every unit has a permanent code, a sourced name, and a record of what is known — including where nothing is known yet.

      Where the rows are

      L6 is the register's centre of gravity. L9 arrived in one run and is already the second largest level, from fifteen countries of a possible 135.

      Where the data comes from

      Share-alike reaches a minority of rows, in 49 of 272 files, and ten countries hold 72% of it. A licence question about “the register” was really a question about fifty files.

      What is not here

      Settlements stop at 170,540. That is the whole gazetteer we can reach; the full 4.8-million-place dump is not obtainable from here. 64,300 are placed, about 65,000 remain.

      No geometry. The register stores none, by design: an outline is derived from its source when something needs drawing, never stored as truth. These maps are drawn from the sources, not from the register.

      231 countries have no depth_real. For those, “not entered” and “does not exist” still look the same — the one confusion the whole design exists to prevent.

      Two countries are entered too deep. Monaco's constitution forms the territory into a single commune and the Vatican's divides it not at all, yet the register holds rows beneath both.

      Open faults

      Every claim here is a claim about ground, so each is drawn from the same polygons the sweep used. Where the register and the map disagree, the map shows where.

      Belarus: a drawing that said north and meant alphabet

      ruled — withdrawn

      Gomel is the southernmost oblast and it sat under NORTHERN. Vitebsk is the northernmost and it sat under SOUTHERN. Sorted alphabetically the six read Brest, Gomel, Grodno, Minsk, Mogilev, Vitebsk — the first three and the last three.

      Ruled: leave the six. 119 rows retired, 117 reissued; the oblasts are Belarus's own first tier now. The validator learned from it too — order is where a row is drawn, and a retired row is never drawn, so rule 6 checks live rows only.

      Slovakia: the same fallback, withdrawn

      3,014 rows entered

      Slovak law builds four rungs and the register held a two-way split no source supports. Withdrawn under the Belarus ruling: the eight kraje are the country's own first tier, with the okresy beneath and 2,927 obce and boroughs below those.

      The district list gives each okres its kraj, so the geometry could be asked whether it agreed. It agrees on all 79.

      Names the source damaged, and the join that repaired them

      closed in both countries

      geoBoundaries' Slovak and Slovene name fields contain no accented character at all, and the letters that carried them are replaced, not stripped. Unlike mojibake this is not reversible: the bytes are gone.

      Solomon Islands: the province the map forgot

      caught in the run

      geoBoundaries draws Malaita as one island, leaving out Ontong Java and Sikaiana, which are Malaita Province. Wards by area therefore handed Luaniua and Pelau to Isabel.

      The constituency layer knows what the province layer forgot, so wards route through it. Malaita came out 33 and Honiara 12 afterwards, which are the published counts; before the fix they were 34 and 10. Honiara was missing from L4 altogether.

      Bratislava and Košice: a unit that does not nest

      ruled

      Bratislava is one obec divided into 17 mestské časti — and it spans five okresy. Košice is one obec across four. The city is bigger than its parent tier and smaller than the tier above, so a containment tree has no slot for it.

      What goes in is what nests: the 39 boroughs sit at L6 under the okres that contains each. The two cities have no row, and that absence is the honest record of a real fact.

      The first cross-cutting unit the register has met. The rule is written: enter the tiling, record the unit that does not tile.

      Monaco and Vatican City: entered below anything that exists

      needs a ruling

      Monaco's Constitution, article 78: le territoire de la Principauté forme une seule commune — and it gives that commune no subdivisions. The Vatican's Fundamental Law of 31 July 2026, article 4, takes the territory whole from the Lateran Treaty and divides it no further.

      The register holds eight Monegasque rows and two Vatican rows at L4. The ground is real; the word administrative was not. They keep their codes. What is open is what they should now say they are.
